Grammar · Comparison lesson

Present Perfect vs Present Perfect Continuous

Result & quantity vs. process & duration — telling the two apart with confidence.

Level B1 60 minutes Task-supported grammar lesson
Main aim

By the end of the lesson, the student will be better able to choose correctly between Present Perfect and Present Perfect Continuous, based on whether the focus is on a result/quantity or on a process/duration.

Subsidiary aims

Clarify meaning through CCQs · practise each form in isolation · contrast the two directly · use both naturally in speech.

1. Lead-in 2. Noticing 3. Present Perfect 4. Pres. Perf. Continuous 5. Contrast 6. Discrimination 7. Speaking 8. Wrap-up
1

Lead-in

4 min
Ask out loud, no writing yet. Just get her talking — both tenses will come up naturally without you naming the grammar.

• What have you been doing this week?

• What have you achieved so far this month?

2

Noticing

6 min
Read the dialogue together. Ask: "How many different grammar forms can you spot? What's different about them?" Don't explain yet — just let her notice there are two patterns.

A: Have you finished the report yet?

B: Almost! I've been working on it all morning, but I've already finished the first two sections.

A: How many pages have you written so far?

B: I've written about ten pages. I've been typing non-stop since 9 a.m.!

A: You've been working so hard today!

B: I know. I've drunk three cups of coffee already.

3

Present Perfect — in isolation

10 min
PP

have/has + past participle. Used for a finished action with a result or fact that matters now — often with an exact quantity (how many / how much / how many times).

  • Is the action finished? (Yes.)
  • Do we know or care exactly when it happened? (No — or it's not the point.)
  • Why mention it now? (Because the result is true right now.)
  • Can we count how many? (Often yes — "three cups", "ten pages".)
1I finish my homework. Can I watch TV now?have finished
2She write three emails this morning.has written
3We never / try sushi before.have never tried
4He lose his keys again!has lost
5you / see the new film yet?Have you seen
6They already / leave the office.have already left
4

Present Perfect Continuous — in isolation

10 min
PPC

have/has + been + verb-ing. Used for a process or activity — often still going on, or only just stopped — with the focus on duration, not on a countable result.

  • Is the action still happening, or did it just stop? (Either — the focus is on the activity itself.)
  • Are we interested in the process or in a finished result? (The process.)
  • Can we usually give an exact number? (Not really — that's not the point.)
  • What question word often triggers this form? ("How long...?")
1It rain since this morning.has been raining
2I learn English for five years.have been learning
3She wait for the bus for twenty minutes.has been waiting
4We work on this project all week.have been working
5you / feel okay lately?Have you been feeling
6He play video games all afternoon — his eyes are red!has been playing

Side by side

8 min
Result (PP)
Process (PPC)
Present PerfectPresent Perfect Continuous
Focus: result / fact / quantity Focus: process / duration / activity
Clues: already, just, yet, ever, never, so far, "how many/how much" Clues: all day, lately, recently, "how long"
I've painted the wall. (it's done — a result) I've been painting the wall. (the process — maybe still unfinished)
Trap: stative verbs (know, want, believe, understand, like, have = possess) never take the continuous form — even with "for/since".
✗ I've been knowing her for years.   ✓ I've known her for years.
Trap: once you give an exact number, switch to Present Perfect, even if it was a long process.
✗ I've been reading three books this month.   ✓ I've read three books this month.
6

Discrimination practice

12 min

Exercise 3 — choose the right form

1I read that book — it was great.have read (result)
2I read that book for two weeks, but I'm only halfway through.have been reading (process)
3She write her thesis for six months now.has been writing (duration)
4She write three chapters so far.has written (quantity)
5We clean the house — it looks great now!have cleaned (result)
6We clean the house all day — we're exhausted.have been cleaning (process)
7He know her since university.has known (stative — no continuous)
8They wait for 45 minutes already.have been waiting (duration)

Exercise 4 — find and fix the mistake

1I've been knowing him for years.I've known him for years. (stative verb)
2She has written her essay for three hours.She has been writing her essay for three hours. (ongoing process)
3We've been finished the project.We've finished the project. (result, not continuous)
4How many books have you been reading this month?How many books have you read this month? (exact quantity → PP)
5He has been lost his phone twice this week.He has lost his phone twice this week. (quantity → PP, no continuous)

Over to her — freer speaking

10 min
Ask these one by one, as a real conversation. Don't correct mid-flow — note mistakes, give feedback after.

2What's the most interesting thing you've done this year?
3How many countries have you visited?
4What have you been working on lately?
5Have you ever tried a food you didn't like? What was it?

Wrap-up — quick self-check

5 min
1. Which sentence focuses on a RESULT?
2. Which sentence focuses on a PROCESS?
3. Which verb type NEVER takes the continuous form?
4. "I've visited Spain ___ times." Which fits?

Last step: ask her to explain the difference in her own words, without looking at the page. If she can say it naturally, the lesson has worked.
