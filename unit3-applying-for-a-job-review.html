2. The cover letter: structure and rules

A cover letter (also called a covering letter) is sent with your CV and highlights the key points from it.

PartWhat to writeUseful language
1. GreetingIf you do not know the name of the person you are writing toDear Sir or Madam
2. The jobSay at the beginning which job you are applying forPlease find attached my CV in support of my application for the position of…
3. Studies and trainingMention any courses you have done which are relevant to the roleI have just completed my degree in… / modules that are relevant to the role
4. ExperienceMention relevant experience and give examples of specific projects you have carried outcustomer-facing experience / projects I have carried out
5. Suitability and aimsShow that you are right for the role and say what you want to developyour suitability for the role / I am keen to gain hands-on experience in this field / I am a team player
6. Previous managementIf you have managed people beforeIn my previous role as (Sales Manager), I led a team of (four people).
7. ClosingThank the reader, then close the letterThank you for taking the time to consider this application. I look forward to hearing from you. Yours faithfully
Greeting and closing must match.
  • You do not know the name: Dear Sir or Madam … Yours faithfully.
  • You know the name: Dear Ms White … Yours sincerely (British English).

3. Key phrases from the unit

Learn them with the pattern that follows.

PhrasePatternExample
be responsible forbe responsible for + noun / -ingYou will be responsible for dealing with customer enquiries.
deal withdeal with + noun
deal, dealt, dealt		Her job involves dealing with customers on the phone.
customer enquiriesanswer / deal with customer enquiries
enquiry (BrE), inquiry (AmE)		She answers customer enquiries by phone and by email.
keen to / keen onkeen to + infinitive
keen on + noun / -ing		I am keen to develop my career.
I am keen on working with clients.
complete a degreecomplete a degree in + subjectI have just completed my degree in Business Studies.
gain experiencegain (hands-on) experience in + areaI am keen to gain experience in this area.
carry outcarry out + a project / a task / researchGive examples of projects you have carried out.
Watch out.
  • The phrase from the job ad is responsible for. The verb respond goes with to: She responded to the enquiry within an hour.
  • Post, position and role mean the job itself. Openings are available jobs (vacancies).
  • Salary is the money you are paid. The benefits package is everything extra the company offers (pension, healthcare plan, subsidised meals).
